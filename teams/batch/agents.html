How MindSuite studies your batch

Much of a legacy system's real work happens at night, in jobs nobody looks at until they fail. A team of 10 agents reads the scheduler's job definitions and run history and finds what the Batch & scheduling card promises: job streams and their order, schedules and batch windows, dependencies between jobs, failures and reruns. Nothing is started or changed.

Four rules the whole system follows

Definitions and history onlyThe agents read exports of job definitions and past runs. No job is started, held or changed.
Passwords never shownCommand lines with passwords are reported as file and line only and never sent to Claude.
Every claim is citedEach finding points to the job definition or history line, or to the findings it is built on.
People decideAgents propose. Your operations team confirms, corrects or rejects each result.

One finding, end to end

  1. The job reader reads the Control-M export: EXTRACT_DELIVERIES at 02:00, then CALC_SETTLEMENT, then SEND_PAYMENTS and PRINT_STATEMENTS.
  2. Job streams turns the waits into one stream, four jobs, three steps deep; Schedules places it in the 23:00–06:00 window.
  3. Failures & reruns finds CALC_SETTLEMENT failing one night in six, rerun each time, and growing from about 68 to 108 minutes in a month.
  4. The migration assessment moves the stream as one unit with the settlement application, after the failures are fixed.
  5. Your operations team confirms the reruns are done by hand at 03:00, which becomes a fact in the inventory.

What is running today

All 10 agents run in the same MindSuite agent backend as the other teams. In MindSuite, open Record evidence → Batch & scheduling → Run the batch & scheduling agent team.

Eight rule-based agents always run and nothing leaves the backend machine. The business-meaning agent runs only with a Claude API key on the backend and your consent for that run.
